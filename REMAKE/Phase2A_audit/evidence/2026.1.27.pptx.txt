Structural Limitation: Why Cad / Lad Cannot Fix UHF Behavior
Key Conclusion
Even when the added element (Cad or Lad) causes 
orders-of-magnitude changes
 in the local branches (
Z6 and Z5
), the 
total impedance remains almost unchanged
.
This is because their influence is 
numerically buried
 by three dominant terms: 
0.5·Z1 ≈ 2.07 
kΩ
, 
0.5·Z2 ≈ 18.7 Ω
, 
Z4 ≈ 37 Ω
These three terms lock the global impedance in the 
tens-of-ohms range
, preventing any effective inductive–capacitive transition at 10⁷–10⁸ Hz.
Structural Interpretation
The current topology is 
structurally stiff
: Z6 is always added to a 
kΩ
-level
 term (0.5·Z1); Z5 is always added to a 
tens-of-ohms
 term (0.5·Z2)
The series arm 
Z4
 is itself a stable, dominant branch
Therefore, even dramatic changes in Z6 or Z5 
cannot compete numerically
.
Additional Observation
Replacing 
Cad with Lad
 leads to the 
same outcome
: Z6 and Z5 still vary strongly, but their effects are again 
completely masked
 by
0.5·Z1, 0.5·Z2, and Z4.
